# The Technocracy — Briefing as of June 1991

---

## What It Is

A transnational intelligence organization operating inside Western intelligence services — primarily the DGSE, with embedded elements in the CIA, Norwegian E-tjenesten, Dutch BVD, and Belgian SGRS. It has no headquarters, no charter, no flag. It uses the resources, authority, and legal cover of its host agencies to pursue objectives that no single government has authorized in full.

It is not a rogue faction. It is not a conspiracy of individuals. It is an **institution** — with generational continuity, operating procedures, internal hierarchy, and a civilizational mission that has survived leadership transitions, political upheavals, and the death of its founder.

---

## Origins

The Technocracy's roots lie in **Charles de Gaulle's transnational Resistance network** during the Second World War. The French Resistance, by operational necessity, was never purely French — it recruited across occupied Europe, built trust networks that crossed every border, and forged bonds between men and women who fought in the dark without flags or uniforms. When the war ended, those networks did not dissolve. They could not. The shared experience was too deep, the institutional knowledge too valuable, the personal loyalties too strong.

De Gaulle understood this. His postwar intelligence architecture — the BCRA evolving into SDECE, the parallel personal networks, the Françafrique system — preserved the wartime networks in institutional form. Under de Gaulle's Fifth Republic (1958–1969), these networks were formalized into something more permanent: a transnational organization embedded inside allied intelligence services, operating with French strategic culture at its core but transcending French national interests.

The transition from SDECE to DGSE in 1982 (under Mitterrand, and later cemented by Philippe de Villiers) provided an institutional reorganization that The Technocracy used to solidify its position within the French intelligence establishment. The expansion into the CIA occurred during the early Cold War, when Gaullist networks and the nascent American intelligence community found common cause against the Soviets. The American branch developed its own operational culture — MKUltra (1953–1973) was a Technocracy project executed through CIA infrastructure — but the organizational DNA remains French.

**As of January 1, 1991, The Technocracy is approximately fifty years old.**

## Current Operations — June 1991

### The Axis (AXYZ) — Umbrella Designation

**The Axis** is the overarching codename for The Technocracy's current global operational framework. The name references the **XYZ** intelligence cooperation of 1939 — the historic moment when Polish cryptographers (Z) shared their Enigma-breaking work with French (X) and British (Y) intelligence at Pyry, near Warsaw. The Technocracy adds **A** for America, creating **AXYZ** — pronounced "Axys," or simply "Axis" in RP English.

The naming is deliberate on every level:

- **XYZ** is the foundational myth of Franco-Polish intelligence partnership — the single act of cooperation that arguably won the Second World War. By invoking it, The Technocracy claims direct lineage from the most consequential intelligence alliance of the 20th century.
- **A for America** — the prefix that transforms a European triad into a global architecture. America joins the front of the alliance, not as its leader but as its extension.
- **"Axis"** — a deliberate reclamation. The Axis powers (Germany, Italy, Japan) were the enemy that XYZ helped defeat. The Technocracy names its own alliance after the concept it destroyed — claiming that the real axis of power, the line around which the world rotates, is not the one history named but the one history hid.

**The four letters — current operational status:**

| Letter | Country | Nature | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | United States | Intelligence (CIA — Technocracy branch) | Active. Compartmentalized from official CIA. Provides funding, technical capabilities, American political cover. |
| **X** | France | Intelligence (DGSE — Technocracy core) | Active. Primary operational vehicle. Silberzahn's seat. |
| **Y** | United Kingdom | Social / aristocratic | Ghost. |
| **Z** | Poland | Intelligence (Spy-State Architecture) | **Active (Mature).** As of December 1991, with the KBP Act active, Node Z has transitioned from a proxy project to a fully sovereign intelligence power. Jelski operates as an equal partner. |

**Y — The Ghost Letter.** Britain is not part of The Technocracy through intelligence channels. MI6 is structurally inseparable from the CIA through the Five Eyes arrangement (UKUSA, 1946) — anything shared with British intelligence flows automatically to the full American apparatus, not just The Technocracy's compartmentalized branch. Bringing MI6 inside would compromise the entire architecture.

Instead, Y is a *social* presence. Members of the British aristocracy — old families with deep ties to continental European nobility, many descended from wartime SOE officers who fought alongside de Gaulle's Resistance — and minor Windsors provide what no intelligence service can: social access at the highest level. Introductions at country houses, seats on the right boards, invitations to the right dinners, connections to the City of London's financial infrastructure through personal relationships rather than institutional ones. A minor Windsor calling a French countess about a weekend in the Dordogne doesn't generate a Five Eyes cable. An earl introducing a Norwegian industrialist to a Polish diplomat at Ascot doesn't appear in any intelligence database.

There is no Y desk. No Y officer. No Y budget line. There are simply people who know the right people, who are sympathetic to certain ideas about European civilisation, and who make introductions when asked politely. Y is present in every room where The Technocracy needs social cover or elite access — and absent from every document, every briefing, every operational file. A ghost, literally: you can feel it, but you cannot point at it.

All specific operations — including Foreuse/Świder — are projects within the AXYZ framework.

### Node Z Evolution: The December 1991 Shift (From Proxy to Partner)

By late December 1991, the internal dynamic of the AXYZ alliance undergoes a fundamental shift. When Krzysztof Bryne-Jelski assumed the premiership in June 1991, Poland (Node Z) was treated by Paris (Node X) as a **proxy project** — an ambitious, highly promising operation (Foreuse), but ultimately one directed and protected by French intelligence and Omani capital.

Six months later, Jelski is no longer just an asset or a junior manager. He has become a **peer**.

The catalyst for this shift is the **Kolegium Bezpieczeństwa Państwa (KBP) Act**, which came into force on December 17, 1991. The KBP architecture (centralizing domestic intelligence, military special operations, and police under a single, prime-ministerial command) gave Jelski a level of domestic executive control that few Western European leaders possess. 

This theoretical power was proven in practice during the **Christmas Eve Anti-Pruszków Operation (Dec 24/25, 1991)**. In a single night, utilizing the new KBP framework, Polish state security obliterated the largest organized crime syndicate in the country, seizing over 600 million PLN and arresting over 600 individuals with surgical precision. 

For Silberzahn, Mitterrand, and de Villiers, this operation was a revelation. It proved that:
1. **The methodology works.** The Israeli-style "spy-state" architecture they envisioned for Poland is fully functional.
2. **Poland is secure.** The internal consolidation of power means Jelski is not vulnerable to ordinary political or criminal destabilization.
3. **Jelski does not need daily micromanagement.** He commands a lethal, highly efficient state apparatus capable of independent, large-scale operations without French hand-holding.

As 1992 begins, Jelski sits at the AXYZ table not as the manager of the Polish project, but as the sovereign commander of the Z Node. Paris must now negotiate with him, not just instruct him. The alliance has become truly multipolar.

#### Oman — The Sultanate (via Y, then far beyond Y)

Oman is not a node. Oman is the **financial backbone, physical infrastructure host, and civilizational co-architect** of The Technocracy. One could argue — and some within the organization quietly do — that the entire Technocracy is, at a certain angle, an Omani plot to reshape the world. This is not entirely wrong.

**Sultan Qaboos bin Said Al Said** is not a sympathizer. He is a full, knowing partner with his own seat at the table, his own vision of the future, and his own reasons for bankrolling the project. He knows The Technocracy by function if not by that specific name. He understands the architecture. He has shaped parts of it. Krzysztof Bryne-Jelski knows him personally — they have met, dined, spoken at length about sovereignty, modernization, and the patient engineering of civilizations. Their relationship is not handler-asset. It is peer-to-peer: two men who think in decades and who view the management of nations as a craft to be mastered.

**How Y became something larger:** The original connection ran through Y — Sandhurst graduates, Dhofar War veterans, British aristocrats who served the Sultan and remained in his circle. But the relationship outgrew Y almost immediately. Once Qaboos understood what The Technocracy was building and saw how his own interests aligned, the British social channel became a relic. The Sultan deals directly with X (Silberzahn, DGSE) and with Z (Krzysztof). Y provided the introduction. Oman provided the empire.

**What Oman provides — the full scope:**

**1. Financial dominance.** The Sultanate is The Technocracy's primary financial contributor. Omani oil revenues, channeled through the Sultan's personal discretion and sovereign wealth instruments, fund operations that no Western intelligence budget could sustain without legislative oversight. A sovereign monarch's personal finances are the most opaque financial instrument on Earth — no parliament approves them, no auditor reviews them, no journalist FOIAs them. The Technocracy's black budget is, to a significant degree, Omani money. This is not charity. It is investment. Qaboos expects returns — not financial, but civilizational.

**2. Physical infrastructure.** Omani territory hosts actual, physical Technocracy facilities:

- **Black sites** — detention and interrogation facilities operating under Omani sovereign authority, invisible to Western legal frameworks. What happens in Oman stays in Oman. No European court, no American congressional oversight committee, no human rights organization has jurisdiction.
- **Training facilities** — remote desert and mountain terrain for operational training that cannot be conducted on European or American soil without attracting attention. The Dhofar region, scarred by the 1960s–70s rebellion but now quiet, provides exactly the environment needed.
- **Secure meeting venues** — Muscat and the Omani interior serve as neutral ground for conversations that cannot happen anywhere else. Oman maintains diplomatic relationships with everyone simultaneously — Iran and Iraq, Israel and Palestine, the West and the Arab world. People who cannot officially be in the same room can meet in Oman without generating a diplomatic incident.
- **Logistics and transit hubs** — Omani ports (particularly Duqm, Salalah, and Muscat) provide maritime access to East Africa, the Indian subcontinent, and the wider Indian Ocean. For a Technocracy that envisions European reengagement with Africa, Omani logistics are the physical pipeline.

**3. Military and intelligence integration.** The Omani Armed Forces (particularly the Royal Army of Oman and the Sultan's Special Force) and the Internal Security Service (ISS) are closely integrated with Technocracy operational structures. This is not the compartmentalized, limited-knowledge relationship that Norway or the Netherlands have. Omani military and intelligence leadership understands who they are working with and why. Joint training, shared intelligence on Gulf and Indian Ocean threats, and coordinated operations in the Horn of Africa are routine, not exceptional.

**4. The "recolonization" blueprint.** The Omanis are the dominant intellectual force behind The Technocracy's vision for Africa and the developing world — and their version is not European neo-colonialism in a new suit. It is something older, more sophisticated, and rooted in their own imperial history.

The Omani Empire was a maritime trading civilization that controlled Zanzibar, large stretches of the East African coast, and trade routes spanning the Indian Ocean for centuries. This was not European-style territorial conquest — it was commercial, maritime, and culturally syncretic. The Omanis built Zanzibar into one of the wealthiest cities in the Indian Ocean world. They intermarried with local populations. They spread Islam — specifically Ibadi Islam — through trade and scholarship rather than conquest.

When The Technocracy discusses "reengagement with Africa," the Omanis hear something very specific: **the restoration of the Indian Ocean trading world they built and lost.** Not European colonialism reheated, but Omani maritime civilization extended — with European technology, European institutional design, and Omani cultural and religious infrastructure.

**5. The Ibadi mission.** This is Oman's own civilizational agenda within The Technocracy, and it is non-negotiable.

**Ibadism** is the third major branch of Islam — older than the Sunni-Shia split, practiced almost exclusively in Oman (~75% of the population), and theologically characterized by:

- **Tolerance and coexistence** — Ibadi jurisprudence historically emphasizes peaceful relations with non-Muslims and with other Muslim traditions.
- **Scholarly rationalism** — Ibadi religious authority is vested in learned scholars (not hereditary clerics or charismatic preachers), emphasizing textual interpretation and debate.
- **Rejection of violent extremism** — Ibadism explicitly rejects the takfiri tradition (declaring other Muslims to be apostates) that underpins Wahhabi/Salafi jihadism.
- **Compatibility with modernization** — Qaboos has demonstrated, through Oman's own transformation from medieval sultanate to modern state, that Ibadism can coexist with secular governance, Western education, and technological development.

The Omanis view the spread of Ibadism as the antidote to the two poisons destabilizing the Islamic world: **Saudi-exported Wahhabism** (which produces jihadism) and **Iranian-exported revolutionary Shiism** (which produces sectarian conflict). Their proposition to The Technocracy is explicit: if Europe wants to stabilize Africa and the Middle East, it needs a religious framework that is Islamic (because Islam is not going away), moderate (because extremism produces chaos), and compatible with Western institutional values (because the project requires cooperation, not subordination). Ibadism is that framework. Oman is its source.

The Technocracy's European members — Silberzahn, Mitterrand, de Villiers, Krzysztof — find this argument persuasive for pragmatic reasons. The Omanis believe it for reasons that are simultaneously pragmatic and genuinely religious. Qaboos is not cynically using Islam as a tool. He believes Ibadism is the correct interpretation of the faith, and he believes its global spread would make the world better. That he also benefits strategically from its spread does not make his conviction less genuine.

#### Andorra — The Sovereign Vault (via X)

Andorra is the node that nobody thinks to look at, which is precisely why it is indispensable.

**The constitutional fact:** The Principality of Andorra is a co-principality — governed jointly by two co-princes: the **Bishop of Urgell** (a Spanish Catholic prelate) and the **President of the French Republic**. As of January 1, 1991, François Mitterrand (succeeded by Philippe de Villiers) is the Co-Prince of Andorra. He is not an ally of Andorra, not an influencer, not a patron. He is its **sovereign**. Personally. By historical right dating to 1278.

**The constitutional void:** Andorra has no constitution. It will not adopt one until March 14, 1993. In January 1991, the principality operates under medieval-era legal arrangements — the *Pareatges* of 1278 and 1288 — supplemented by custom and the personal authority of the co-princes. There is no formal separation of powers, no independent judiciary in the modern sense, no constitutional court, no bill of rights. The co-princes have enormous personal discretion over Andorran affairs. Mitterrand and de Villiers do not need to manipulate Andorran institutions. There are, in any meaningful modern sense, almost no institutions to manipulate. They *are* the institution.

**What nobody knows about Andorra — and what The Technocracy exploits:**

**1. Banking opacity.** Andorran banks in the early 1990s operate in a regulatory vacuum. Andorra is not a member of the European Community. It is not subject to EU banking directives, international reporting standards, or external auditing requirements. Banking secrecy in Andorra in 1991 is not Swiss-style (regulated secrecy within a legal framework) — it is **pre-regulatory** (secrecy by absence of rules). Money enters Andorran banks and effectively ceases to exist in the international financial system. No one traces it because there is nothing to trace it with. There are no forms. There are no reports. There is a bank, a vault, and a handshake.

The Technocracy maintains accounts in Andorran banks that serve as the primary European-side financial clearing house. Omani money arrives through channels that pass through Andorran banking and emerges clean, untraceable, and denominated in whatever currency the operation requires. This is not money laundering in the criminal sense — the co-prince of the country personally authorizes the arrangement. It is sovereign finance, conducted within the legal authority of the head of state. Whether that distinction is moral rather than merely legal is a question The Technocracy does not ask.

**2. Sovereign territory beyond scrutiny.** Andorra is 468 square kilometers of Pyrenean mountain terrain — remote, physically difficult to access, and of zero strategic interest to anyone who is not skiing or buying duty-free electronics. No foreign intelligence service monitors Andorra. No journalist investigates Andorran affairs. No international organization audits Andorran governance. It is, in intelligence terms, a **blind spot the size of a country.**

The Technocracy uses this:
- **Secure archives** — documents, records, and materials too sensitive for French or American soil are stored in physical vaults on Andorran territory, under the sovereign protection of a co-prince who knows exactly what they contain.
- **Meeting venues** — Andorran mountain residences, unreachable except by specific roads through the Pyrenees, provide meeting locations that are simultaneously within easy reach of France and Spain yet invisible to surveillance. No embassy monitors Andorra. No signals intelligence installation covers it. People enter and leave through mountain passes, and nobody records who they are.
- **Legal safe harbor** — actions taken on Andorran soil under the co-prince's authority are, legally, actions taken by a sovereign state. They cannot be investigated by French courts, Spanish courts, or European courts without the co-prince's consent. Mitterrand and de Villiers will not consent.

**3. The 1993 constitution — a controlled transition.** Andorra will adopt its first written constitution on March 14, 1993. This will formally establish democratic institutions, limit the co-princes' powers, and bring Andorra into the modern international legal order. In The Technocracy's timeline, this is not a loss of control — it is a **controlled transition**: Mitterrand and de Villiers shape the constitutional text to *appear* to limit their authority while embedding institutional structures that preserve The Technocracy's access. The new constitution creates a Andorran parliament, an independent judiciary, and formal democratic processes — all genuine, all functional — while ensuring that the co-prince retains specific reserved powers and that the banking secrecy framework survives in modified form. He is not losing Andorra. He is formalizing his control in a structure that will survive his death.

**4. Population and deniability.** Andorra has approximately 50,000 residents. The entire country has fewer people than a medium-sized Polish town. In a population this small, The Technocracy does not need a network — it needs perhaps a dozen people: the *Síndic General* (head of the Andorran parliament), two or three senior bankers, a handful of administrative officials, and the French *Veguer* (the co-prince's personal representative in Andorra, appointed by Mitterrand/de Villiers). These individuals manage The Technocracy's Andorran interests not because they are ideologically committed to the project but because the co-prince asked them to, and in Andorra, the co-prince's request is not distinguishable from a command.

**The Andorra-Oman financial circuit:** Omani sovereign wealth → Andorran banking (opacity) → European operational accounts (clean). This is the financial circulatory system of The Technocracy. Oman provides the money. Andorra washes it — not in the criminal sense, but in the sovereign sense. France receives it. The entire circuit operates under the personal authority of two sovereigns (Qaboos and Mitterrand/de Villiers) and is invisible to every regulatory and oversight mechanism in the Western world.

#### Israel — The Mirror (via X, Z, and deep history)

Israel is not a node of The Technocracy. Israel is The Technocracy's **proof of concept** — the demonstration that the methodology works.

**The Zionist project as template:** A small group of intellectuals and activists, driven by civilizational conviction, decided to *build a nation from scratch*. They engineered mass migration, created institutions ex nihilo, designed an economy, constructed a military doctrine, revived a dead language, and imposed a national identity on a territory through sheer force of organized will — all deliberately, over decades, against the resistance of empires and the skepticism of the world. The Kibbutz was technocratic socialism in miniature. The Haganah was a state-building apparatus that preceded the state. The entire enterprise was a civilizational engineering project pursued by a dedicated elite who believed they knew where history needed to go and were willing to force it there.

The Technocracy looks at Israel and sees itself in a mirror — the same methodology, the same confidence, the same willingness to subordinate democratic norms to civilizational necessity. Israel is, in many operational respects, already the **spy-state** that The Technocracy intends to build in Poland: a democracy where intelligence agencies (Mossad, Shin Bet, Aman) hold structural influence over policy that no election can fully override, where the security establishment's strategic prerogatives define the boundaries within which civilian politics operates. The Technocracy does not need to invent this model. It already exists. It is called Israel.

**The French-Israeli intelligence axis:**

The DGSE-Mossad relationship is one of the oldest and deepest bilateral intelligence partnerships in the Western world — and it runs far deeper than the official Franco-Israeli diplomatic relationship suggests.

- **The nuclear bond:** France built Israel's Dimona reactor. The Israeli nuclear weapons program — undeclared, unacknowledged, the worst-kept secret in international relations — exists because France provided the technology, the expertise, and the political cover in the 1950s and early 1960s. This is a Technocracy-era achievement, whether anyone within the organization uses that word or not. The men who authorized French nuclear assistance to Israel were the same men who built The Technocracy's French institutional infrastructure. The debt is remembered on both sides.
- **The 1967 rupture — and what survived it:** De Gaulle broke with Israel publicly after the Six-Day War, imposing an arms embargo and pivoting French Middle East policy toward the Arab world. The diplomatic relationship cooled dramatically. The intelligence relationship did not. DGSE and Mossad continued cooperating at levels that the political relationship could not publicly sustain — sharing assessments, coordinating on specific threats, maintaining personal contacts between senior officers. By 1991, the official relationship is "correct but cool." The shadow relationship is warm.
- **The Technocracy connection:** Specific senior Mossad figures — not the institutional Mossad, but individuals within it — understand that their DGSE counterparts represent something larger. They do not know The Technocracy by name or by full architecture. They know that their French contacts have unusual resources, unusual reach, and unusual autonomy. They find this useful. They do not ask too many questions, because Mossad officers are trained to recognize when a relationship is more valuable uninvestigated.

Two Polish speakers, one building a spy-state in Warsaw, the other running one in Jerusalem, conversing in the language of the country whose destruction forged both their peoples' modern identities and which is now being rebuilt by one of them.

The symbolism is not lost on either man. They do not discuss it.

The Technocracy has established relationships with **multiple Securitate fragments** — not through a single handler-asset relationship (as with Krzysztof in Poland) but through a web of contacts cultivated by the DGSE during the Cold War and expanded during the post-revolutionary chaos. In Poland, the architecture depends on one man (Krzysztof) and one ally (Syrop).
