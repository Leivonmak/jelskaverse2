# AMANTIS PJ-50A

## First-Generation Continuous ASP Graphene Manufacturing Line

**Manufacturer:** AMANTIS / Thales industrial systems consortium
**Process:** Proces Jelskiej — direct roll-to-roll PECVD with Acoustic Stabilisation and Purification
**Series:** PJ-50A, Series I
**Primary installation:** AMANTIS Manufaktura nr 2, Świdnik
**Entry into serial service:** 2017
**Primary role:** Continuous manufacture of high-quality monolayer graphene on qualified flexible substrates for military structural, aerospace, naval and electronic applications.

## Product classes

The PJ-50A can produce several grades, but each machine is normally qualified for a narrow group of substrates and products.

### Structural grade

Used for:

* armour interleaves;
* aramid and ceramic composites;
* anticorrosion systems;
* electromagnetic shielding;
* lightning-strike layers;
* naval and aerospace laminates.

Priority is given to coverage, adhesion, mechanical continuity and low defect variation across large areas.

### Electronic grade

Used for:

* sensors;
* transparent electrodes;
* conformal antennas;
* strain gauges;
* specialised flexible electronics.

Priority is given to carrier mobility, sheet-resistance uniformity, low contamination and low active-defect density.

### ASP reference grade

Produced in limited quantities for:

* research;
* calibration;
* defect spectroscopy;
* development of new substrate recipes;
* comparison between Świdnik and Mandelieu process standards.

## Process gases

Primary process gases are:

* ultra-high-purity methane;
* hydrogen;
* argon;
* nitrogen for purging and inert handling;
* recipe-specific trace precursor gases.

The ASP chamber may receive extremely small quantities of methane, acetylene or mobile carbon-bearing species when true vacancies require an external carbon atom. ASP cannot create missing matter.

All gases undergo final point-of-use purification. The Świdnik site may receive bulk methane from the regional natural-gas network, but reactor supply is purified and certified to semiconductor-process standards.

## ASP chamber

The ASP chamber contains:

* segmented piezoelectric transducer arrays;
* nonlinear phononic coupling assemblies;
* independently phased power-amplifier channels;
* laser interferometric vibration measurement;
* Raman and electrical feedback;
* trace-carbon injection;
* vibration-isolated web support;
* a classified defect-spectrum and waveform library;
* adaptive process-control computers.

ASP is the complete stabilisation and purification operation.

DLAPP—Defect-Localised Anharmonic Phonon Pumping—is one mechanism used within ASP. It transfers acoustic-pump energy through nonlinear mode coupling and concentrates it in defect-localised vibrational modes. Other ASP functions include strain control, guided adatom transport, acoustic cleaning and process self-termination after a defect signature disappears.

---

## Control architecture

The line uses three control layers:

1. **Safety control**
   Independent hardwired interlocks, gas isolation, emergency shutdown and pressure protection.

2. **Conventional process control**
   Web speed, tension, temperature, pressure, plasma power, gas flow and cooling.

3. **Jelska adaptive controller**
   Reduced-order kinetic models, defect classification, predictive recipe adjustment and ASP waveform generation.

Atomistic simulations are performed offline and converted into reduced models and spectral libraries. The entire moving sheet is not simulated atom by atom in real time.

The classified ASP controller is air-gapped from ordinary plant networks.

## Maintenance philosophy

The PJ-50A is modular. Activation, growth, cooling, ASP and metrology sections can be isolated and serviced independently.

Typical maintenance cycle:

* daily automated health checks;
* weekly optical and electrical calibration;
* monthly short maintenance stop;

# Operating Sequence of the AMANTIS PJ-50A

A production run begins with a qualified substrate reel entering the secure preparation area. The reel may contain thin metallic ribbon, a ceramic-coated carrier or another material approved for that particular line. It is cleaned externally, identified by machine-readable tags and sampled for incoming inspection before a transporter carries it to the load end of the machine.

## 1. Load cassette

The reel is mounted inside a sealed cassette at the left end of the line. The circular door closes around it, and the cassette is evacuated separately from the main process chambers.

Motors slowly rotate the reel while sensors measure:

* web thickness;
* edge position;
* surface defects;
* reel eccentricity;
* tension;
* remaining length.

The broad ribbon enters the machine through a narrow vacuum slit. Guide rollers make minute corrections to prevent it drifting sideways. An accumulator system maintains stable tension when the drive changes speed.

The operator sees the reel turning through the large pressure-rated window, but once the web passes into the machine, it is visible only through selected process ports.

## 2. Surface activation

The ribbon first passes through a degassing and activation zone.

Moderate heating removes absorbed water and volatile contamination. A low-pressure argon plasma then bombards the surface, removing residual organic material and creating chemically active sites.

This chamber glows faintly violet. Its purpose is not to deposit graphene. It creates a predictable and reproducible surface on which graphene can begin to form.

The controller adjusts plasma power to avoid roughening the carrier. Too little activation produces poor adhesion; too much creates damage that later appears as nucleation centres.

## 3. Controlled nucleation

In the nucleation chamber, methane, hydrogen and argon are admitted in tightly controlled proportions.

The plasma breaks the methane into reactive carbon-bearing fragments. A small number of those fragments attach to the prepared surface and form the first stable graphene nuclei.

This is one of the most delicate stages. Too many nuclei produce many small crystal domains and therefore many grain boundaries. Too few leave gaps before the web reaches the end of the growth reactor.

The controller therefore balances:

* precursor concentration;
* hydrogen etching;
* plasma energy;
* web temperature;
* residence time;
* local strain.

A low-amplitude acoustic field may be used here to bias surface transport and discourage uncontrolled secondary nucleation. This is part of the wider ASP process philosophy, although the main purification operation occurs later in the dedicated ASP chamber.

## 4. Multi-zone PECVD growth

The web enters the eighteen-metre growth reactor.

Internally, this is not one uniform chamber. It is a chain of separately controlled plasma zones connected by differential-pumping sections. Each zone performs a slightly different part of the growth process.

In the first zones, existing graphene nuclei expand laterally. In later zones, the growing domains approach one another and join. Hydrogen-rich plasma removes unstable carbon formations and suppresses amorphous deposits. The final growth zones discourage a second graphene layer from forming over the first.

The long purple glow visible through the main viewport comes from the plasma extending across the moving ribbon. The graphene itself is effectively invisible.

Sensors continuously monitor:

* plasma emission;
* gas composition;
* web temperature;
* optical reflectivity;
* early Raman response;
* pressure distribution.

The adaptive controller predicts how the film is developing farther upstream and alters conditions before a deviation becomes a rejected section several metres later.

At two metres per minute, the web spends several minutes inside the complete growth system. When it leaves, the substrate carries a continuous graphene layer, but the lattice still contains vacancies, bond rotations, grain-boundary irregularities and residual strain.

## 5. Thermal consolidation

The web next enters a dark, non-plasma thermal chamber.

Here the newly formed film is held at controlled temperature under a neutral or reducing atmosphere. Weakly attached carbon species are removed, the lattice relaxes and the adhesion between graphene and substrate stabilises.

This is a conventional annealing step rather than the ASP breakthrough. It improves the material generally, but it cannot selectively address every remaining atomic defect.

The ribbon is kept under carefully regulated tension. A small tension error at this temperature could create wrinkles that survive every later stage.

## 6. Controlled cooling

The web cannot be taken directly from growth temperature to the near-room-temperature ASP chamber.

It passes through several cooling zones, each slightly colder than the previous one. The machine adjusts roller speed and tension to compensate for thermal contraction of the substrate.

Cooling is deliberately gradual to prevent:

* cracking;
* wrinkling;
* delamination;
* frozen-in strain;
* misalignment at the ASP entry.

By the end of this section, the web is stable, flat and close enough to ambient temperature for precision acoustic treatment.

## 7. ASP chamber

The ribbon enters the mechanically isolated ASP chamber.

This chamber is wider, heavier and more rigid than the PECVD modules. The web is supported across a precisely controlled acoustic coupling surface. Beneath it are segmented transducer arrays, each driven independently in amplitude and phase.

Before treatment begins, Raman, electrical and interferometric sensors estimate the population and distribution of remaining defects. The controller compares those signatures with the classified defect library and selects a sequence of acoustic waveforms.

DLAPP operates here.

The megasonic signal is not treated as a direct atomic hammer. It pumps energy into the substrate–graphene system. Nonlinear coupling transfers that energy through higher-frequency modes until it becomes concentrated in a vibrational mode localised around a particular defect configuration.

A Stone–Wales defect may rotate back into a lower-energy bond arrangement. A mobile carbon atom may be guided toward a vacancy. A metastable grain-boundary kink may reconstruct.

Where an atom is actually missing, the chamber introduces a minute carbon-bearing feedstock. ASP supplies the selectivity and activation; the gas supplies the missing matter.

The process is naturally self-limiting. When the defect is repaired, its characteristic local vibration disappears and that location ceases to absorb the selected waveform efficiently.

The chamber repeatedly measures, treats and measures again while the web moves through it. To a visitor, nothing dramatic is visible. On the process displays, defect populations decline and quality maps change from warning colours to qualified green.

## 8. Cleaning and final passivation

After ASP, the surface receives a final low-damage cleaning.

Dry acoustic streaming, controlled desorption and mild plasma treatment remove weakly bound reaction products and remaining process contamination. A wet megasonic treatment is used only for substrate systems qualified to tolerate it.

The graphene may then receive:

* a protective molecular passivation layer;
* an encapsulating dielectric;
* a temporary transport film;
* a bonding layer for later composite manufacture.

Electronic-grade material is normally protected before it is exposed to ordinary factory air.

## 9. Inline metrology

The finished web passes beneath a full-width inspection bridge.

Optical heads scan laterally while the ribbon moves forward. Electrical rollers or non-contact probes measure conductivity. Raman instruments sample the D, G and 2D bands. Cameras detect particles, folds, edge defects, cracks and wrinkles.

The metrology system does not simply approve or reject an entire reel. It creates a continuous map.

A local defect at metre 814.37 may be marked for exclusion while the surrounding hundreds of metres remain saleable. Downstream cutting and lamination equipment reads the same map and avoids the affected coordinates.

Selected samples are removed automatically for laboratory analysis by AFM, TEM, XPS or detailed electrical testing.

## 10. Unload cassette

The qualified web enters the final sealed cassette and is wound onto a clean receiving core.

A protective interleaf may be added during winding. The machine maintains tension carefully so that the graphene is not creased or compressed unevenly.

The completed roll is purged with inert gas, sealed and assigned a digital material passport containing:

* substrate batch;
* complete process history;
* ASP treatment record;
* defect map;
* quality class;
* intended downstream use;
* security classification where applicable.

The cassette is then disconnected from the line and moved to secure storage or the adjoining conversion plant.

What leaves the machine appears almost identical to what entered it: a broad roll of reflective or translucent material. The difference is a nearly continuous carbon crystal one atom thick, grown directly on its working substrate and inspected at every point along its length.
