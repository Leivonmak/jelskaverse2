# AMANTIS ATHENA Family
## Second-Generation Additive Lattice Manufacturing Platform

**Status:** Canonical technical overview  
**First industrial introduction:** 2023, AMANTIS Manufaktura nr 2, Świdnik  
**Technology generation:** Second generation  
**Predecessor:** PJ-series continuous graphene manufacturing lines  
**Core process framework:** ASP — Acoustic Stabilisation and Purification  
**Key physical mechanism:** DLAPP — Defect-Localised Anharmonic Phonon Pumping

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The **ATHENA family** is AMANTIS’s second-generation platform for additive manufacturing at the micro-, nano-, and atomic scales.

Its public description is deliberately simple:

> **Three-dimensional printing from methane, using sound as the atomic printhead.**

That phrase is useful, but incomplete. ATHENA does not fire methane molecules from a nozzle, nor does it rely on acoustic wavelengths as small as atoms. Instead, it combines:

- methane and other hydrocarbon precursors;
- remote plasma chemistry;
- hydrogen-assisted bond control;
- ASP-controlled phononic fields;
- DLAPP-based local activation;
- nanoscale scaffolds and temporary termination chemistry;
- continuous atomic and spectroscopic feedback;
- automated correction of malformed bonds.

The first-generation PJ lines grew graphene through conventional PECVD and then repaired and purified it in a dedicated ASP chamber. ATHENA changes that sequence fundamentally. ASP is no longer only a post-growth treatment. It participates throughout synthesis, controlling where precursor fragments arrive, which bonds form, which allotrope is produced, and whether an incorrect local structure is retained, repaired, or removed.

ATHENA therefore treats **bond hybridisation, lattice topology, layer count, defect population, and local three-dimensional geometry as manufacturing parameters**.

## 2. Name and Meaning

### French institutional designation

**ATHENA — Assemblage Toposélectif Hydro-Exalté pour l’Édification Nanométrique et Atomique**

### English technical designation

**ATHENA — Atomically Targeted, Hydrogen-Enhanced Nanoscale Assembly**

### Informal engineering description

> **Additive lattice manufacturing using methane-derived carbon and ASP-controlled phononic fields.**

The term **toposélectif** refers to selection by location and local atomic environment. ATHENA does not simply energise a whole surface. It identifies chemically distinct sites and directs reactions toward selected local configurations.

Hydrogen is described as **enhancing** the process because it serves several roles even when it is absent from the final product:

- assisting methane cracking;
- removing unstable carbon deposits;
- terminating incomplete edges;
- stabilising temporary intermediates;
- enabling graphane formation;
- reversing malformed carbon bonds;
- protecting growing graphyne chains;
- temporarily supporting metastable penta-graphene structures.

A common AMANTIS summary is:

> **Carbon establishes the structure; hydrogen controls when and where that structure may form.**

## 3. Relationship to Earlier AMANTIS Technologies

### PJ-series

The first-generation **PJ-series** uses the *Proces Jelskiej*:

1. prepare a moving substrate;
2. grow graphene through roll-to-roll PECVD;
3. consolidate and cool the sheet;
4. repair and purify it in an ASP chamber;
5. inspect, passivate, and rewind it.

Example:

- **AMANTIS PJ-50A**

### ASP

**ASP — Acoustic Stabilisation and Purification** is the overall process framework for:

- acoustic modulation of nucleation;
- guided surface transport;
- selective defect treatment;
- atomically assisted repair;
- acoustic cleaning;
- adaptive process control.

ASP can refer both to the treatment process and to the dedicated chamber in first-generation machines.

### DLAPP

**DLAPP — Defect-Localised Anharmonic Phonon Pumping** is one physical mechanism used within ASP.

DLAPP uses a shaped megasonic pump signal, nonlinear mode coupling, and defect-localised vibrations to concentrate energy into selected reaction coordinates. It does not create missing matter and does not replace chemistry. It provides local activation and selectivity.

### ATHENA

ATHENA is not a single mechanism. It is a complete second-generation machine architecture that combines:

- ASP;
- DLAPP;
- plasma chemistry;
- hydrogen control;
- phononic near-field addressing;
- nanoscale additive manufacturing;
- scaffolded three-dimensional growth;
- real-time structural verification.

The clean hierarchy is:

> **PJ is a first-generation manufacturing process. ASP is the acoustic control and treatment framework. DLAPP is a physical mechanism. ATHENA is the second-generation machine architecture and product family.**

## 4. Core Principle of the Second-Generation Process

ATHENA transforms gaseous carbon precursors into controlled atomic structures.

The process can be simplified into seven repeating steps:

1. **Map** — determine the present atomic and chemical configuration.
2. **Dose** — introduce the required carbon, hydrogen, dopant, or termination species.
3. **Trap** — create a local phononic reaction field.
4. **Bond** — activate the intended sp, sp², or sp³ bond configuration.
5. **Verify** — inspect the newly formed region.
6. **Correct** — reverse malformed bonds, remove excess atoms, or fill missing sites.
7. **Lock** — stabilise the completed region before continuing.

These steps occur in parallel across large areas. ATHENA does not normally place every atom serially. Most of a lattice is produced through controlled self-assembly, with atomic-scale intervention used selectively where ambiguity, defects, interfaces, or unusual topology require it.

That distinction is essential to industrial throughput.

## 5. Precursor Preparation

### Primary carbon source

The standard carbon feedstock is ultra-high-purity methane.

Methane enters a remote plasma reformer, where it is converted into a controlled distribution of reactive species such as:

- C;
- CH;
- CH₂;
- C₂;
- C₂H;
- acetylene-like fragments;
- recipe-specific carbon radicals.

The machine then separates, filters, or enriches the fragment distribution required for the active recipe.

### Additional process species

Depending on the material, ATHENA may also use:

- atomic hydrogen;
- molecular hydrogen;
- acetylene;
- ethylene;
- aromatic or ethynyl molecular precursors;
- isotopically selected carbon;
- nitrogen, boron, sulfur, selenium, or other dopant species;
- temporary edge-termination molecules;
- sacrificial scaffold precursors.

Methane remains the sovereign bulk carbon source, but not every ATHENA material is assembled directly from methane fragments alone. Graphyne-family materials, for example, may require internally synthesised C₂-rich or molecular building blocks.

## 6. The Phononic Printhead

ATHENA’s defining component is the **programmable phononic printhead**.

It contains a dense metasurface of independently addressable micro- and nanoresonators. These resonators do not create atom-sized free-space sound waves. Instead, their overlapping near fields produce highly localised reaction zones known informally as **sonic voxels**.

The printhead performs four principal functions:

- concentrates precursor species near selected sites;
- changes local adsorption and migration barriers;
- favours a chosen bonding pathway;
- assists removal or reconstruction when the measured result differs from the design.

The sound field is supported by:

- electric-field control;
- remote plasma excitation;
- local heating where required;
- optical or electron-assisted metrology;
- hydrogen-mediated temporary chemistry;
- ASP and DLAPP feedback.

Sound is the coordinating and selective mechanism, not the only energy source.

## 7. Printing Geometries

ATHENA operates in three main geometries.

### 7.1 Surface growth

A web, panel, wafer, or rigid component moves beneath one or more printheads.

The machine produces:

- monolayers;
- multilayers;
- patterned conductive regions;
- local allotrope changes;
- nanoribbons;
- pores;
- strain-engineered areas;
- embedded interfaces.

This is the fastest operating mode and the direct successor to first-generation graphene web processing.

### 7.2 Scaffolded three-dimensional growth

The machine prints onto:

- a sacrificial molecular scaffold;
- a temporary metal or ceramic nanolattice;
- a pre-printed nanoscale support;
- a previously completed carbon structure.

After synthesis, the scaffold is:

- dissolved;
- sublimated;
- chemically removed;
- acoustically detached.

This mode enables:

- porous carbon nanolattices;
- vertical interconnects;
- nanoscale mechanical structures;
- three-dimensional membranes;
- impact-dissipating metamaterials;
- molecular filtration systems.

### 7.3 Self-supporting edge growth

For short bridges, tubes, folds, arches, and shells, ATHENA can grow outward from anchored edges.

The machine continuously measures:

- local strain;
- edge geometry;
- bond completion;
- out-of-plane displacement;
- risk of collapse or reconstruction.

Large unsupported structures still require scaffolding. ATHENA is not a universal matter compiler.

---

## 8. Structural Verification and Error Correction

Every ATHENA machine combines process synthesis with metrology.

Depending on precision grade, it may use:

- Raman spectroscopy;
- near-field optical spectroscopy;
- electron diffraction;
- tunnelling or atomic-force probes;
- local electrical measurements;
- interferometric surface mapping;
- defect-spectrum matching;
- isotopic and chemical analysis.

When an incorrect structure is detected, the machine may:

- dehydrogenate a site;
- re-hydrogenate it in a different conformation;
- rotate a malformed bond;
- remove an excess carbon fragment;
- supply a missing atom;
- reopen a partially closed molecular linkage;
- rebuild a local grain boundary;
- alter temporary scaffold geometry.

The completed region is stabilised only after it passes recipe-specific verification.

## 9. Modularity

ATHENA installations are built from standard process bays, normally four to six metres long.

A complete machine may include:

- reel, wafer, panel, or scaffold handling;
- methane purification;
- remote plasma reforming;
- precursor separation;
- hydrogen and dopant sources;
- phononic printhead bays;
- electrostatic control;
- local thermal control;
- ASP correction zones;
- inline metrology;
- scaffold removal;
- passivation;
- encapsulation;
- finished-product handling.

Material capability is changed through combinations of:

- precursor cassettes;
- printhead cassettes;
- phononic metasurfaces;
- scaffold modules;
- metrology packages;
- software libraries;
- process recipes.

Not every machine supports every material. ATHENA is modular, but industrial systems remain product-qualified and specialised.

## 10. Machine Naming Convention

The canonical format is:

> **ATHENA–[material]–[width]–P[precision]–[format]**

Example:

> **ATHENA-GR-1000-P1-W**

Meaning:

- **GR** — graphene;
- **1000** — 1,000 mm active working width;
- **P1** — electronic precision;
- **W** — continuous web format.

### 10.1 Material codes

| Code | Material |
|---|---|
| **GR** | Graphene |
| **GH** | Graphane, graphone, and patterned hydrogenated graphene |
| **GY1** | Graphyne |
| **GY2** | Graphdiyne |
| **GY3** | Higher graphyne-family allotrope with three consecutive acetylenic links |
| **PG** | Penta-graphene |
| **CX** | Mixed carbon allotropes in a single structure |

### 10.2 Format codes

| Code | Format |
|---|---|
| **W** | Continuous flexible web |
| **P** | Rigid panel or wafer |
| **V** | Volumetric or scaffolded 3D printing |
| **X** | Hybrid, multi-allotrope system |

### 10.3 Precision grades

| Grade | Effective chemical addressability | Primary uses |
|---|---:|---|
| **P0 Structural** | 50–100 nm voxels | Coatings, armour films, bulk membranes |
| **P1 Electronic** | 5–10 nm | Sensors, conductors, antennas, large-area electronics |
| **P2 Quantum** | Approximately 0.5–1 nm | Nanoribbons, pores, junctions, controlled defect arrays |
| **P3 Atomic** | Individually distinguishable lattice sites | Quantum devices, research, penta-graphene, exact interfaces |

Precision grade describes effective chemical addressability, not the wavelength of the pump signal.

A P3 machine does not build an entire large sheet one atom at a time. It invokes single-site control selectively while most of the surrounding lattice forms through parallel self-assembly.

# 11. Graphene Machine Family

Graphene is the easiest and fastest ATHENA product because a planar sp² lattice naturally propagates from a correctly prepared edge.

## 11.1 ATHENA-GR-2000-P0-W

**Format:** 2,000 mm continuous web  
**Precision:** P0 Structural

Primary products:

- armour interleaves;
- aircraft lightning-strike layers;
- naval barrier films;
- thermal spreaders;
- electromagnetic shielding;
- high-volume composite reinforcement.

The machine prioritises:

- coverage;
- continuity;
- adhesion;
- mechanical uniformity;
- high throughput.

It uses atomic-scale intervention only where required to preserve process stability or repair major defect populations.

## 11.2 ATHENA-GR-1000-P1-W

**Role:** Standard second-generation electronic graphene line  
**Format:** 1,000 mm continuous web  
**Precision:** P1 Electronic

Primary products:

- patterned conductive films;
- integrated antennas;
- transparent electrodes;
- sensor grids;
- flexible circuits;
- locally controlled multilayers;
- large-area electronic surfaces.

This machine can vary local layer count, crystal orientation, and conductive geometry during growth.

## 11.3 ATHENA-GR-500-P2-W

# 12. Graphane and Hydrogenated-Graphene Family

Graphane is produced by forming graphene and converting selected carbon sites from sp² to sp³ bonding through ordered hydrogen attachment.

The principal challenge is controlled access to both faces and the prevention of irregular hydrogen clustering.

## 12.1 ATHENA-GH-1000-P1-W

**Role:** Industrial graphene–graphane patterning  
**Format:** 1,000 mm continuous web  
**Precision:** P1 Electronic

The machine contains:

- graphene synthesis heads;
- hydrogen-plasma modules above and below the web;
- reversible dehydrogenation heads;
- dual-sided ASP metrology.

Its most common product is not a uniform roll of pure graphane. It prints mixed sheets in which:

- graphene acts as conductor;
- graphane acts as insulation or wide-band-gap material;
- partially hydrogenated graphene acts as a tunable semiconductor.

## 12.2 ATHENA-GH-500-P2-W

**Role:** High-resolution hydrogenated flexible materials  
**Format:** 500 mm continuous web  
**Precision:** P2 Quantum

Primary products:

- flexible logic;
- chemical sensors;
- radiation-sensitive films;
- reversible electronic surfaces;
- hydrogen-storage research materials;
- programmable graphene–graphane interfaces.

Selected patterns can be written, erased, and rewritten through controlled hydrogenation and dehydrogenation.

## 12.3 ATHENA-GH-300-P3-P

**Role:** Exact graphane, graphone, and hydrogen-defect engineering  
**Format:** 300 mm panel or wafer  
**Precision:** P3 Atomic

Capabilities include:

- face-selective hydrogenation;
- chair, boat, and other local conformations;
- designed hydrogen vacancies;
- exact graphene–graphane boundaries;
- isolated magnetic or quantum-active sites;
- one-sided graphone structures.

# 13. Graphyne-Family Machines

Graphyne-family materials contain both sp² carbon regions and sp-bonded acetylenic chains. They cannot be produced by lightly modifying graphene.

ATHENA graphyne systems therefore include a **C₂ precursor module** that converts methane-derived carbon into acetylene-rich or molecular building blocks before lattice assembly.

## 13.1 ATHENA-GY1-500-P2-W

**Role:** Continuous graphyne membrane and semiconductor production  
**Format:** 500 mm continuous web  
**Precision:** P2 Quantum

Primary products:

- graphyne membranes;
- anisotropic semiconductor sheets;
- molecular sieves;
- gas-selective films;
- ion-selective films;
- advanced catalytic supports.

The system alternates aromatic-ring formation with insertion of acetylenic links. ASP verifies chain length, orientation, and closure.

## 13.2 ATHENA-GY2-1000-P1-W

**Role:** Industrial graphdiyne production  
**Format:** 1,000 mm continuous web  
**Precision:** P1 Electronic

Primary products:

- porous separation membranes;
- catalyst supports;
- energy-storage electrodes;
- environmental filtration films;
- large-area semiconducting membranes.

Graphdiyne has the strongest path to early industrial scale within the graphyne family because its molecular assembly is easier to organise through repeating precursor chemistry.

## 13.3 ATHENA-GY1-300-P3-P

**Role:** Precision graphyne device fabrication  
**Format:** 300 mm panel or wafer

Primary products:

- exact pore arrays;
- graphyne nanoribbons;

- atomically defined catalytic sites.

Its process chamber combines molecular synthesis, nanoscale printing, and atomic error correction.

# 14. Penta-Graphene Machine Family

Penta-graphene is the most speculative ATHENA material.

It is a buckled carbon sheet based on pentagonal topology and mixed sp²/sp³ bonding. It is prone to reconstruction and cannot initially be produced on an ordinary flat moving web.

The process requires:

- dynamically shaped nanoscale scaffolds;
- controlled out-of-plane buckling;
- mixed hybridisation control;
- temporary hydrogen termination;
- continuous ASP correction.

## 14.1 ATHENA-PG-150-P3-P

**Role:** First repeatable penta-graphene synthesis  
**Format:** 150 mm panels  
**Precision:** P3 Atomic  
**Production mode:** Batch

The substrate acts as an active phononic template. Its nanoscale geometry imposes the required pentagonal pattern while temporary hydrogen termination stabilises incomplete edges.

Primary uses:

- fundamental research;
- military materials research;
- auxetic membranes;
- extreme-strain sensors;
- nanoscale impact structures.

## 14.2 ATHENA-PG-300-P3-P

**Role:** Preproduction penta-graphene sheets  
**Format:** 300 mm panels  
**Precision:** P3 Atomic

Primary products:

- high-band-gap electronic materials;
- impact-resistant nanolaminates;
- unusual mechanical sensors;
- auxetic reinforcement layers;
- high-strain structural membranes.

It remains a panel system rather than a fast web line.

## 14.3 ATHENA-PG-150-P3-V

**Role:** Volumetric penta-carbon nanolattice printing  
**Format:** 150 mm scaffolded volume  
**Precision:** P3 Atomic

Primary products:

- nanoscale springs;
- auxetic lattices;
- molecular shock absorbers;
- high-strain hinges;
- curved penta-carbon shells;
- three-dimensional carbon metamaterials.

This is the ATHENA machine that most closely resembles a true three-dimensional printer.

## 17. Development Timeline

### 2023

**ATHENA-GR-1000-P1-W** enters industrial service in Świdnik.

It demonstrates that ASP can control lattice growth and local topology during synthesis rather than merely repair completed graphene.

### 2024–2025

- ATHENA-GR-2000-P0-W enters structural production.
- ATHENA-GR-300-P3-P is installed in Mandelieu.
- ATHENA-GH-500-P2-W and ATHENA-GH-1000-P1-W begin graphene–graphane device production.
- First operational graphene–graphane flexible circuits are qualified.

### 2026–2027

- ATHENA-GY2-1000-P1-W enters graphdiyne production.
- ATHENA-GY1-300-P3-P produces exact graphyne devices.
- First mixed graphene–graphyne membranes enter defence and industrial trials.
- P2 phononic printheads become routine on 500 mm systems.

### 2028–2029

- First repeatable penta-graphene domains are demonstrated.
- ATHENA-PG-150-P3-P enters laboratory service.
- First scaffolded three-dimensional penta-carbon structures are produced.
- Atomic-precision correction becomes reliable across selected 150–300 mm work areas.

### 2030 onward

- ATHENA-PG-300-P3-P enters preproduction service.
- ATHENA-CX-300-P3-X becomes the flagship multi-allotrope platform.
- Larger hybrid systems are considered if penta-graphene and graphyne yield remain stable.
- ATHENA architecture begins expanding beyond carbon allotropes into selected two-dimensional semiconductors, nitrides, and thin-film metamaterials.

## 18. Industrial and Strategic Significance

ATHENA represents a shift from manufacturing materials to manufacturing **lattice states**.

Its strategic value lies in the ability to control:

- local conductivity;
- band gap;
- pore geometry;
- bond hybridisation;
- defect identity;
- isotopic composition;
- mechanical response;
- quantum-active sites;
- three-dimensional nanoscale topology.

A first-generation PJ line produces an exceptionally uniform graphene sheet.

An ATHENA machine can produce a sheet or component in which different regions perform different functions because the local atomic structure itself has been designed.

Potential applications include:

- integrated conductive and insulating carbon electronics;
- conformal antennas;
- quantum sensors;
- atomically precise filtration membranes;
- reconfigurable surfaces;
- catalytic arrays;
- radiation-hard devices;
- nanoscale mechanical metamaterials;
- ultralight impact structures;
- multifunctional aerospace skins;
- atomically engineered armour interleaves;
- molecular-scale energy systems.

## 19. Realism Boundary

ATHENA remains a hard-science-fiction platform rather than a universal fabricator.

### Plausible extensions

The most defensible capabilities are:

- advanced graphene synthesis;
- graphene–graphane patterning;
- high-quality graphdiyne;
- defect-controlled graphyne structures;
- nanoscale scaffolded carbon printing;
- selective atomic correction;
- local hybridisation control.

### Deeper speculative capabilities

The most speculative areas are:

- stable large-area penta-graphene;
- reliable single-site control across industrial work areas;
- high-throughput three-dimensional atomic printing;
- universal multi-allotrope transitions within one workpiece.

### Explicit limitations

ATHENA:

- does not create matter;
- does not bypass chemistry;
- does not arbitrarily position atoms in free space;
- works best on surfaces, edges, interfaces, and thin structures;
- requires material-specific models and process libraries;
- depends on scaffolds for most large 3D objects;
- cannot freely transform any carbon allotrope into any other without suitable intermediate chemistry;
- remains vulnerable to contamination, thermal drift, incorrect precursor balance, and model error;
- becomes slower as precision and topological complexity increase.

Its power comes from combining controlled self-assembly with selective local correction—not from serially placing every atom.
